# Watching your grandchild's screen time — a plain guide

This is for a parent or grandparent who wants to keep an eye on a child's laptop and phone use.
No apps to install, nothing technical. If a step doesn't match what you see, you can't break
anything — just ask the person who set this up.

## What this is

The child's laptop and phone quietly keep a tally of which apps and websites they use, and for how
long. You can look at that tally on a web page, from your own phone or computer, whenever you like.
It's open — the child can see it's there — and you can only look; you can't change anything on
their devices.

## Getting in

1. You'll be given a **web address** (a link), a **name**, and a **password**. Keep them somewhere
   safe, like you would for your bank.
2. Open the link in any web browser (the one you already use is fine).
3. Type the **name** and the **password**, and press the button to sign in.

That's it. You're looking at the child's day.

### Make it one tap next time (optional)

- **On an iPhone or iPad:** open the link in Safari, tap the **Share** button (the square with an
  arrow), then **Add to Home Screen**. A little button appears with your other apps.
- **On an Android phone:** open the link in Chrome, tap the **⋮** menu (top right), then **Add to
  Home screen**.
- **On a computer:** save the page as a bookmark, or keep the tab open.

Next time, just tap that button and sign in.

## What you'll see

- **Day** — the child's day so far: which apps and websites, and how long on each.
- **Week** — the same, for the whole week.
- A green dot means a device is being used right now.
- Time in a **private** or **incognito** window shows only as "Private" — you see that it
  happened and for how long, never which pages.

If there's more than one device (say a laptop and a phone), you can pick which one to look at, or
see them together.

## Good to know

- **You can only look.** You can't lock the device, change settings, or remove anything. If
  something needs doing, tell the parent who set this up.
- **The child knows it's there.** This isn't hidden from them. It's for looking after them openly,
  not spying.
- **If it won't load:** check your internet first (open any website). If the internet works but
  this page doesn't, tell the person who set it up — it's a quick fix for them.
- **If it says the name or password is wrong:** type it slowly and carefully; the password may have
  a capital letter or a number. If it still won't let you in, ask for a fresh password — the parent
  can make you a new one in a moment.

## If in doubt

Take a photo of whatever the screen shows and send it to the person who set this up. A picture
tells them exactly what's happening — that's the most helpful thing you can do.
